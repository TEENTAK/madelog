## SHOT-BY-SHOT SEQUENCE

**SHOT 1 — 0.0–2.0 seconds | Aeri's accusation**

Medium two-shot from a fixed, eye-level camera. Aeri sits beside the man on the sofa and looks directly up at him with a suspicious, serious expression.

**Aeri:** "You've been petting another dog."

Keep Aeri's entire small body and the man's upper body spatially readable. Do not enlarge Aeri to fill the frame unnaturally.

**SHOT 2 — 2.0–3.5 seconds | The denial**

Cut to a medium shot favoring the man, with Aeri's small head or body visible in the foreground when composition permits. He looks surprised and raises his hands defensively.

**Man:** "What? No."

His expression is nervous but restrained, consistent with natural sitcom acting.

**SHOT 3 — 3.5–4.5 seconds | The evidence**

Cut to a speaker-focused shot of Aeri at a realistic small-dog scale. She maintains eye contact with the man, ears alert, with an unwavering and suspicious expression.

**Aeri:** "I can smell her."

Preserve her exact character identity. Do not alter her muzzle, eye spacing, coat pattern, or body proportions to exaggerate the expression.

**SHOT 4 — 4.5–6.3 seconds | The confession**

Cut back to the man in a medium shot. He glances away and makes a small explanatory gesture, appearing guilty.

**Man:** "Okay, it was just one belly rub."

Aeri remains nearby, watching him.

**SHOT 5 — 6.3–8.2 seconds | The revelation**

Cut to a fixed three-quarter shot showing the man, Aeri, and the doorway. The woman appears in the doorway and looks at the man with disbelief.

**Woman:** "Bella? The Samoyed next door? Again?"

The man turns toward the woman. Aeri briefly redirects her gaze toward the speaker, reacting to the revelation.

**SHOT 6 — 8.2–10.0 seconds | The punchline**

Cut to a medium close-up of Aeri, with the man partially visible at the edge of the frame. Aeri shifts her gaze back toward him, her expression changing from suspicion to stunned disbelief.

**Aeri:** "Again???"

Hold the final reaction briefly. End on Aeri's intense stare and the man's guilty silence.

## CAMERA, EDITING & PERFORMANCE

Use fixed, eye-level cameras with a natural 35–50 mm lens perspective. Follow classic multi-camera sitcom grammar with medium two-shots, speaker-focused medium shots, and reaction shots.

Change the composition according to the active speaker and the emotional information revealed by each line. The shot should favor the speaking character while preserving enough spatial context to understand the interaction.

Use direct cuts only. No camera movement, zooms, handheld footage, or extreme close-ups. Maintain deep focus so the characters and surrounding set remain clearly visible.

Dialogue must follow the specified order and be synchronized to the correct speaker. Use concise, conversational delivery and brief comedic pauses. The humor comes from the contrast between Aeri's tiny size and serious attitude, the man's defensive denial, and the woman's final revelation.

No subtitles, captions, timecodes, labels, speech bubbles, or text overlays. No background music. Use subtle room tone and restrained sitcom audience laughter only after the final punchline.

## VISUAL MOOD — VERBATIM

Photorealistic live-action in the style of a 1980s American multi-camera studio sitcom, with the warm, natural, lived-in character rendering associated with Friends. Do not reproduce the original cast's likenesses or 1990s wardrobe.

Bright, high-key, low-contrast studio lighting with a broad, warm-white diffused frontal key and generous fill from both sides. Evenly illuminated faces, soft shadows, and minimal brightness separation between subjects and background. Preserve shadow detail, slightly lifted blacks, controlled highlights, and naturally warm skin tones without yellow or orange casts.

A palette of peach, warm yellow, wood brown, and denim blue, with moderately rich, natural saturation. Tangible studio-built interiors featuring patterned wallpaper, wood paneling, velvet upholstery, and period-appropriate lamps. Preserve realistic material textures without artificial aging or faded retro filters.

Subtle, fine, evenly distributed 35mm film grain. Natural skin texture, individual hair strands, and visible fabric weave. Clean, defined details without excessive sharpening, beauty smoothing, soft-focus haze, or artificial glow.

CHARACTERS
Role-appropriate 1980s everyday clothing and hairstyles. Maintain consistent facial features, hairstyles, wardrobe, accessories, and signature colors across reference images and generated shots.

CAMERA & FRAMING
Locked-off, eye-level cameras with natural 35–50mm lens perspective. Frontal or 45-degree three-quarter views, framed exclusively as waist-up medium shots or chest-up medium close-ups. Deep focus keeps characters and surrounding sets clearly visible.
